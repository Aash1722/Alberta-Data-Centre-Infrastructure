# Alberta Data Centre Infrastructure: The "Picks & Shovels" Opportunity

**A market research perspective for private equity** · September 2026

Market research examining how Alberta's data centre buildout creates secondary demand for
small and lower-middle-market infrastructure and service businesses — the contractors who
build and maintain the facilities, rather than the operators who run them.

---

## The research question

> How could the growth of data centres in Alberta create opportunities for small and
> lower-middle-market businesses that support the data-centre ecosystem?

The report deliberately focuses on the supply chain rather than data centre operators
themselves, which are globally financed and largely out of reach for Canadian
lower-middle-market capital.

---

## Key findings

**The binding constraint is deliverable power, not demand.** The Alberta Electric System
Operator reports requested data centre load above 20,600 MW against provincial peak demand
of roughly 13,000 MW. Its interim allowance for new large loads — 1,200 MW through 2028 —
is fully allocated. Roughly 6% of requested load can connect this decade without new
generation or transmission. That constraint is what redirects capital toward on-site
generation and electrical infrastructure.

**The market moved from proposal to committed capital in 2026.** Meta broke ground in July
2026 on a 1 GW campus in Sturgeon County representing more than C$13 billion, paired with
the C$4.6 billion, 932 MW Greenlight Electricity Centre. The general contractor is a joint
venture of two Edmonton-headquartered firms.

**The supplier base is measurably fragmented.** Statistics Canada data shows 4,530 Alberta
employer establishments in building equipment contracting (NAICS 2382) as at 2025. Of
these, 97.7% employ fewer than 100 people, and only 104 province-wide have the headcount to
credibly staff a hyperscale scope.

**The recurring-revenue premise deserves scepticism.** At Comfort Systems USA (NYSE: FIX),
the clearest listed comparable, technology work reached 58% of revenue in H1 2026 and
backlog grew from $8.1bn to $14.1bn in twelve months — but service and maintenance was
still only about 6% of revenue, with service projects a further 5%. The boom is, so far,
overwhelmingly a project-revenue phenomenon.
